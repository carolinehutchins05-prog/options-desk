Week ahead

November 23 to 27, 2026

Stocks enter Thanksgiving week in a correction. The S&P 500 fell 3.7% Friday to 6,910 and lost 8.6% on the week, its worst since April 2025, while the Nasdaq fell 10% and the Dow lost 8%. Nvidia dropped 14% Friday after Reuters reported that regulators were examining its accounting, and chip stocks fell hard with it. Money moved into Treasuries, with the 10 year yield down to about 4.9% from 5.15% a week earlier, and the VIX closed near 36. Almost all of the US data lands Wednesday morning, led by October PCE, the last major inflation report before the December 8 to 9 Fed meeting. Dell on Tuesday night is the first big test of AI spending since the rout. US markets are closed Thursday, and on Friday stocks close at 1:00 PM ET and bonds at 2:00 PM ET.

Biggest day of the week

Wednesday Nov 25: PCE inflation, GDP, durable goods, claims and the Beige Book

Six US releases land between 8:30 AM and 2:00 PM ET on the last full session before Thanksgiving. Core PCE is expected at 0.3% for the month and 3.2% for the year, up from 3.1%. With stocks in a correction and December hike odds slipping to about 92%, the question is whether inflation is firm enough to keep the Fed hiking into a falling market. Expect thinner holiday trading, which can exaggerate moves.

S&P Global flash PMIs (Nov)

ConsensusManufacturing 54.5, services 55.6PriorManufacturing 56.0 flash, services 56.1 final

The first broad look at November business activity. October factory activity was the best since 2022, but prices also jumped. Watch the price components.

How markets could react

Strong activity with hot prices would lift yields and the dollar and could add to the pressure on tech. A moderate slowdown with softer prices would be the friendliest mix for stocks and bonds. A sharp drop could stir growth worries in a market that is already nervous.

4:00 PMHigh impact

Fed speaker: Williams (New York Fed)

ConsensusNonePriorNone

The only scheduled Fed speaker found for the week, and a permanent voter. The Fed goes quiet from midnight Saturday ahead of the December meeting. He recently said more hikes will likely be needed.

How markets could react

If he repeats the call for hikes and plays down the selloff, short yields and the dollar could rise and tech could stay under pressure. Any mention of tighter financial conditions would be read as a door opening to a hold, which would help growth stocks and small caps. About 92% odds of a quarter point hike are already priced.

PCE inflation, personal income and spending (Oct)

ConsensusCore PCE 0.3% m/m, 3.2% y/y. Headline 0.3% m/m, 3.8% y/y. Income 0.4%, spending 0.4%PriorCore 0.3% m/m, 3.1% y/y. Headline 0.5% m/m, 3.6% y/y. Income 0.2%, spending 0.5%

The Fed's preferred inflation gauge. Forecasts come from CPI and PPI, so most of the number is already known. The Cleveland Fed nowcast matches consensus.

How markets could react

A core reading of 0.4% would push hike odds back toward 100%, revive talk of a half point and lift the 2 year yield and the dollar, with tech and small caps most exposed. A 0.2% would lower yields and help growth stocks, homebuilders and gold. An in line number is already priced and would likely shift attention back to the selloff itself.

8:30 AMMedium

GDP second estimate and corporate profits (Q3)

Consensus3.4% to 3.5%Prior3.4% first estimate

An update of third quarter growth plus the first look at profits. Second quarter growth was 2.2%. Watch consumer spending and the price measures.

How markets could react

An upward revision with firm prices would support more hikes and lift yields. A downgrade to consumer spending would help bonds but worry cyclical stocks. Usually secondary to PCE at the same time.

8:30 AMMedium

Initial jobless claims (week of Nov 21)

Consensus200,000 to 201,000Prior203,000

Moved up a day for Thanksgiving. Continuing claims rose to 1.763 million, the highest since late August.

How markets could react

Claims near 200,000 would confirm a tight job market and keep the hike on track. A jump above 215,000 or another rise in continuing claims would rally bonds and lower hike odds. Holiday weeks can be noisy.

8:30 AMMedium

Durable goods orders (Oct)

Consensus0.4% to 0.5%, ex transportation 0.5%Prior1.8%, ex transportation 0.5%

Orders for long lasting goods. Core capital goods orders are a window into business and AI equipment spending.

How markets could react

Strong core orders would calm fears that AI spending is cracking and help industrials and chip equipment. A weak reading would add to the AI worry and weigh on both. Aircraft orders can distort the headline.

10:00 AMLow

New home sales (Oct)

Consensus649,000 to 650,000Prior676,000

Sales of newly built homes with mortgage rates above 7%. Builder confidence just hit its lowest since 2012.

How markets could react

A miss would weigh on homebuilders but help bonds. A surprise gain would support builders and home improvement names. Limited effect on the broad market.

The last note sale of the week, in thin trading before the holiday.

How markets could react

Wider reports of price increases would back a hike and lift short yields. More mentions of weak demand or slower hiring would help the case for patience and support growth stocks.
