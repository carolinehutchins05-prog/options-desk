Week ahead

October 26 to 30, 2026

Rates are the story this week. The Fed raised rates a quarter point in September and is expected to hold on Wednesday while keeping the door open to another hike in December. That decision lands hours before Microsoft and Alphabet report, and Thursday brings the first read on third quarter growth, September inflation, and Amazon's earnings. Friday is quiet on US data.

WednesdayOct 28

3 macro21 earnings

Economic news

2:00 PMHigh impact

FOMC rate decision, then Chair Warsh press conference

ConsensusHold at 3.75 to 4.00%Prior25 bp hike in Sep, unanimous

94 of 100 economists in a Reuters poll expect a hold. Futures put the chance of a hike this week near 9 to 10%. Watch the vote split: Hammack, Logan and Kashkari voted for a hike in July and could dissent again. Midterm elections are Nov 3.

How markets could react

A hold is already priced, so the reaction comes from tone and the vote. Hawkish (hike dissents, a clear December signal): yields and the dollar rise, tech and small caps lag, banks and energy hold up. Dovish tilt: relief rally in growth stocks. A surprise hike would hit stocks hard. The decision comes before Microsoft and Alphabet report, so it sets how their results are received.

8:30 AMLow

Trade balance and wholesale inventories (Sep)

Trade and inventories are the swing factors in Thursday's GDP estimate.

How markets could react

A big change in the trade gap moves GDP forecasts a few tenths, which can shift Thursday's expectations.

GlobalMedium

Bank of Canada, RBNZ, UK Autumn Budget

ConsensusBoC hike to 2.50% (about 80% priced), RBNZ hike to 3.00%PriorBoC at 2.25%

Central banks abroad are tightening too. The UK budget is expected to leave about 15 billion pounds of headroom.

How markets could react

More hikes abroad reinforce the global tightening theme. A surprise hold from either bank would soften the dollar and give the Fed some cover.

ThursdayOct 29

4 macro24 earnings

Economic news

8:30 AMHigh impact

Q3 GDP, advance estimate

Consensus3.0% annualizedPrior2.2% in Q2

The Atlanta Fed GDPNow tracker sits at 3.5%, led by a jump in business equipment spending. Trade and inventories are the swing factors.

How markets could react

Strong growth with sticky inflation is not good news in a hiking cycle: a number at or above 3.5% pushes yields up and weighs on rate sensitive stocks. A print near 2.5% eases hike fears but a much weaker one revives recession talk.

8:30 AMHigh impact

Personal income and outlays, Sep PCE

ConsensusHeadline +0.5% m/m, 3.6% y/y. Core +0.3% m/m, 3.1% y/yPriorHeadline +0.3%, 3.4% y/y. Core +0.2%, 3.0% y/y

The Fed's preferred inflation gauge. It comes after the Fed decision, but it drives December hike odds. The Cleveland Fed nowcast matches consensus at 3.1% core and 3.6% headline.

How markets could react

Core at 0.4% or more would all but lock a December hike: yields up, tech and small caps down. Core at 0.2% would be a relief rally. An in line print should be muted because it is largely priced.

8:30 AMMedium

Initial jobless claims

Prior194,000

The four week average is 197,800. Claims below 200,000 point to a tight labor market.

How markets could react

Another low reading supports the hawkish case. A jump above 220,000 would be the first real sign of labor cracks and would help bonds.

GlobalMedium

ECB decision, Tokyo CPI

ConsensusECB hold

The ECB is not expected to hike given French bond market stress. The focus is on French spreads. Tokyo CPI could firm up a December hike at the Bank of Japan.

How markets could react

A wider French spread would hit European banks and the euro. A hot Tokyo print supports the yen.
