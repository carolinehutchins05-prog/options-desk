Week ahead

November 9 to 13, 2026

Stocks enter the week on the back foot. The S&P 500 fell 2.68% last week to 7,376.86, the Nasdaq dropped 3.37% and the Dow slipped 0.94%, as AI names sold off after midterm wins for candidates who back limits on data center building. A blowout October jobs report added 165,000 jobs versus about 60,000 expected and pulled unemployment down to 4.1%, sending the 10 year yield to about 4.87% and lifting December hike odds to roughly 78% to 80%. This week tests both stories. October CPI lands Tuesday morning and CoreWeave reports that evening, Cisco and Applied Materials report Thursday after the close, and PPI closes the week Friday. Bond markets are shut Wednesday for Veterans Day while stocks trade normally, and a busy slate of Fed speakers will react to every print.

Biggest day of the week

Tuesday Nov 10: October CPI at 8:30 AM ET, then CoreWeave after the close

With a hot jobs report already pushing hike odds near 80%, the CPI print decides whether the Fed has room to wait. Consensus looks for a cooler 0.3% headline and 0.2% core after September's 0.6% jump, so an in line or soft number could steady bonds and tech, while a hot core would cement a December hike. Hours later, CoreWeave reports at the center of worries over AI buildout financing, with options implying a move of about 20%.

Consumer Price Index (Oct)

ConsensusHeadline 0.3% m/m, core 0.2% m/m. No official y/y figurePriorHeadline 0.6% m/m, core 0.3% m/m

The week's main event. October 2025 prices were never collected during the government shutdown, so BLS will not publish a standard year over year rate, and the monthly change carries all the weight. Watch shelter, used cars and core services.

How markets could react

A hot core print of 0.4% or more would push the 2 year yield and the dollar higher, lock in a December hike and hit tech, small caps and housing stocks. An in line or soft 0.2% would ease some of the near 80% hike pricing, pull yields lower and could spark a relief rally in beaten up AI names. Markets already price a hike, so a cool print has more room to surprise.
