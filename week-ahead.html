Week ahead

November 2 to 6, 2026

The first half of the week leaned hawkish on data and mixed for stocks. ISM manufacturing beat at 55.5, though prices paid cooled to 75.2, and JOLTS openings jumped to 7.52M, topping every forecast. Apple and Palantir both beat but fell, Apple on its margin outlook, while AMD beat with data center revenue up 97% and rose more than 7% after hours. The S&P 500 slipped Monday, then snapped a three day losing streak Tuesday on a chip rally. Overnight, Democrats were projected to win the House and the Senate, and stock futures fell, led by tech, as the 10 year yield dropped to near 4.91%. Still ahead: ADP, ISM services and the Treasury refunding today along with Qualcomm and Arm after the close, a Bank of England hike on Thursday, and the October jobs report on Friday.

Friday Nov 6: the October jobs report at 8:30 AM ET

ISM Manufacturing (Oct)

Consensus54.6 to 54.7, prices paid 77Prior54.5, prices paid 77.9

Came in at 55.5 versus about 54.6 to 54.7 expected, the tenth straight month of expansion, with production at 59.0, the highest since November 2021. Prices paid eased to 75.2 from 77.9, below the 77 consensus but still high, and employment slipped to 52.4.

How markets could react

Stocks split on Monday. The Dow rose about 0.4% as oil posted its sharpest drop in three months on hopes for US and Iran diplomacy, while AI selling pulled the Nasdaq down about 1.1% and the S&P 500 down about 0.5% for a third straight loss.

11:15 AMLow

Fed's Goolsbee, Q&A at the BIS

The Chicago Fed president is moderating, and is not a voter this year, so this may not carry a policy view.

How markets could react

Little market impact is likely unless the comments stray directly onto December.

2:00 PMMedium

Senior Loan Officer Survey (Q3)

Banks reported tighter standards on net for all commercial real estate loan categories and weaker demand for them, a reversal from easing in July. Mortgage demand also weakened while mortgage standards held broadly steady.

How markets could react

It was not a market mover. Tighter real estate lending is a modest headwind for regional banks and fits the view that higher rates are starting to bite.

3:00 PMMedium

Treasury financing estimates

Treasury cut its fourth quarter borrowing estimate to $618B, $10B below the August projection, because it started with more cash. Excluding that cushion, the need was $24B higher. It sees $800B of borrowing in the first quarter of 2027.

How markets could react

The smaller headline figure eased supply worries going into Wednesday's refunding, and long term yields had no major reaction that day.

10:30 PMMedium

Reserve Bank of Australia decision

ConsensusHike to 4.85%, odds about 79% to 88%Prior4.60%

The RBA hiked to 4.85% as expected, its fifth hike this year and the highest rate since 2008, but the vote was split 6 to 3 with three members wanting to hold.

How markets could react

The split vote read as less hawkish than the hike itself, and some economists now see a December pause as more likely. It added to the global tightening theme without a surprise.

ISM Services (Oct)

Consensus54.7 to 54.8Prior54.9

Services make up most of the US economy, and the survey's prices and employment parts are the details to read ahead of Friday.

How markets could react

A beat with hot prices would push yields higher and strengthen December hike bets, a headwind for growth stocks. A softer reading with cooling prices would help tech and small caps, unless it is weak enough to raise growth worries.

ADP employment (Oct)

Consensus65,000 to 80,000Prior90,000

A private payrolls preview for Friday, though it is an imperfect guide to the official report.

How markets could react

A big beat could firm up payroll expectations and lift yields. A print near zero would add to labor softening concerns and support bonds.

8:30 AMMedium

Treasury quarterly refunding

ConsensusBarclays expects no change to auction sizes

The Treasury sets auction sizes for the coming quarter, and any change in its guidance matters as much as the sizes themselves.

How markets could react

Hints of larger auctions would steepen the curve and hurt rate sensitive stocks. Steady sizes are the expected outcome and should pass quietly.

8:30 AMLow

Trade balance (Sep)

Consensus$108.0B deficitPrior$105.6B deficit

The gap feeds into revisions to third quarter growth, which came in at 3.4% in the first estimate.

How markets could react

A large change could shift GDP revision estimates by a few tenths, but it is rarely a market mover alone.

GlobalMedium

Eurozone flash CPI, Riksbank, Brazil, Poland

ConsensusEurozone CPI 3.9% to 4.0%, core 2.2% to 2.5%. Riksbank hike to 2.00%PriorEurozone CPI 3.8%, core 2.5%. Riksbank 1.75%

Eurozone inflation rose to 4.0% from 3.8%, at or a tenth above forecasts and the highest since September 2023, driven by energy. The core figure was not confirmed at the time of writing. How markets could react

Another energy driven jump keeps an ECB hike in December in play, which supports the euro and European yields. Watch whether core stayed steady, since that decides how much of the move is energy alone.

Bank of England decision and Monetary Policy Report

ConsensusHike to 4.00%, 7 to 2 votePrior3.75%, 6 to 3 vote to hold in Sep

This would start a tightening cycle, and about 98% is priced. Governor Bailey speaks at 7:30 AM ET. Analysts expect pushback against market pricing of more than four hikes over the next 12 months.

How markets could react

The hike itself is priced, so the vote split and guidance will drive the reaction. Pushback on the hike path would soften the pound and help gilts and UK stocks. A hawkish vote or no pushback would lift gilt yields and could spill into global bonds.

8:30 AMMedium

Initial jobless claims

Consensus195,000Prior194,000

The four week average is 197,800, a very low level that sits oddly next to weak payroll growth.

How markets could react

Another reading under 200,000 keeps the low firing story and the hawkish case alive. A jump above 220,000 would be a real change, helping bonds and weakening December hike odds.

With strong growth and little hiring, productivity may look solid. Unit labor costs are the inflation angle.

October jobs report

ConsensusPayrolls 55,000 to 65,000. Unemployment 4.2%. Hourly earnings +0.3% m/m, 2.9% y/yPriorPayrolls 29,000. Unemployment 4.2%. Hourly earnings +0.1% m/m, 3.0% y/y

September missed badly and prior months were revised down by 60,000 in total. The three month average is about 50,700. Watch revisions and wages as closely as the headline.

How markets could react

A strong number with firm wages would nearly lock in a December hike: yields and the dollar up, tech and small caps down, banks more resilient. A negative or very weak number would pull hike odds down and rally bonds, though stocks could stumble if it raises growth fears. An in line report is largely priced, so a muted move is the likeliest outcome.

10:00 AMMedium

University of Michigan sentiment, preliminary (Nov)

Consensus48.2Prior48.0, year ahead inflation expectations 4.7%

Sentiment is very low. The inflation expectations figure is what the Fed will watch.

How markets could react

A rise in expectations above 4.7% would add to hawkish pressure and lift yields. A decline would be welcome relief for bonds, and any post election swing in sentiment should be read cautiously.

Times varyMedium

Fed speakers Williams, Musalem, Bowman

Williams speaks at 4:30 AM ET, Musalem at 9:10 AM ET and Bowman at 12:15 PM ET. Musalem and Bowman get the first word after the jobs report.

How markets could react

Williams carries the most weight as a permanent voter, so any comment on December could move rates. Comments after payrolls could either confirm or fade the market's first reaction.

Canada jobs, eurozone retail sales, BoJ's Ueda

ConsensusCanada +15,000. Eurozone sales 0.6% y/yPriorCanada minus 68,000. Eurozone sales 0.8% y/y

Canada reports at 8:30 AM ET, eurozone sales at 5:00 AM ET, and Ueda gives an IMF lecture at 11:00 AM ET.

How markets could react

Another poor Canadian report would cut December hike odds there, about 55% now, and weaken the Canadian dollar. A hawkish Ueda would support the yen and could pressure carry trades.
